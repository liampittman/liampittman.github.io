I map the systems that shape civic life,

making hidden infrastructure visible and accountable.

My name is William Pittman, and I'm a data journalist based in south Mississippi. Since 2020, my investigative data work has exposed systemic failures in Mississippi's election infrastructure-research cited by the NAACP Legal Defense Fund in federal voting rights litigation. This includes spatial analysis that revealed how polling place changes affecting 65,000 voters were systematically unreported by stete officials, with relocations disproportionaly impacting Black and Hispanic communitites.

My research has been cited by the NAACP Legal Defense Fund and other civil rights organizations in voting rights litigation. I've also drawn national attention and earned multiple journalism awards. My practice combines spatial analysis, investigative research, and interactive visualization to make complex civic systems legible to the public. I aim to use this space to publish methodologies, code, and datasets openly, contributing to the infrastructure of accountability journalism.

That accountability extends to acknowledging both that the land I work on is unceded Choctaw, Chickasaw, and Biloxi territory and my own complicity as a descendant of early French colonizers and enslavers. This lineage catalyzes the critical precision and stubborn hope with which I approach my home state's systemic challenges, and compels my support for the freedom from oppression for and return of sovereignty to Indigenous peoples everywhere. The time is now: Land Back and Free Palestine.
